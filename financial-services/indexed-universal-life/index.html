Indexed Universal Life (IUL)

Explore index crediting, floor and cap provisions, policy expenses, cash value, loans and lapse risk without assuming illustrated performance will happen.

Explore the essentials

Index-linked crediting is not investing directly in an index.

IUL is a type of universal life insurance. Interest may be credited according to a formula tied to a referenced index. The policy is not a direct purchase of stocks in the index, and insurance charges can reduce cash value even in a year with a credited interest floor.

  • Review the index, participation rate, cap and spread applicable to the strategy.
  • Understand the difference between credited interest and net policy cash-value change.
  • Evaluate guaranteed and non-guaranteed illustrations separately.
  • Review loan interest, policy costs, premium funding and lapse risk.

Understand the tradeoffs

What deserves scrutiny?

IUL involves more variables than a single interest rate.

Cap, spread & participation

Contract formulas may limit how much of an index movement is credited, and terms may change within contract bounds.

Premium funding

Higher or lower contributions can materially affect long-term illustrations and lapse risk.

Insurance costs

Age-related charges, fixed expenses and riders may affect net value over time.

Loans & withdrawals

Borrowing or withdrawing can reduce benefits, accrue interest and create possible tax or lapse issues.

Common questions

Read beyond the illustration

The product details determine the answer.

Is an IUL policy a stock market investment?

No. The credited interest method may reference a financial index, but the policyholder is not directly investing the policy's account value in that index.

Are illustrated returns guaranteed?

No. Illustrations may show non-guaranteed scenarios, and actual crediting and costs can differ.

If the index falls, does a 0% crediting floor prevent a market-linked negative interest credit?

For the defined index-crediting strategy, a 0% floor generally means a negative index movement does not result in a negative indexed-interest credit for that crediting period. This is distinct from ongoing policy charges, withdrawals or loan costs, which can still reduce net cash value. Contract terms govern the actual treatment.

Can I use IUL for retirement?

Some people discuss cash-value access as part of broader financial planning, but suitability, tax treatment, funding, charges and loan risk require contract-specific professional review.
