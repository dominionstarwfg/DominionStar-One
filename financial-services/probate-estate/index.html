What is probate?

Understanding the court process.

Probate generally concerns proving a will or establishing the authority to administer an estate. Whether a court proceeding is needed depends on local law, asset ownership and estate circumstances.

A will and probate are different

A valid will sets out instructions for assets covered by it, but having one does not necessarily mean court involvement can be avoided.

No will: intestacy

When someone dies without a valid will, local intestacy laws usually govern who has the right to inherit estate property and who can administer it.

Executor or estate trustee

An authorized representative may need to identify assets, pay appropriate debts and distribute estate property under applicable law.

Some assets pass outside an estate

Certain jointly held assets and policies with properly designated beneficiaries may pass by other rules; results vary by jurisdiction.

Documented estate stories

Two real cases. One lesson: clarity matters.

These are publicly reported events, not fictional client testimonials. Their outcomes are specific to the courts and estates involved.

Michigan · 2018–2023

Aretha Franklin: competing wills

After Aretha Franklin died in 2018, two handwritten documents dated 2010 and 2014 became the subject of a probate dispute. In July 2023, a Michigan jury found the 2014 will discovered in her couch valid. The case illustrates why clear, accessible, appropriately executed estate documents matter.

Read AP coverage ↗

Minnesota · 2016–2022

Prince: an estate without a will

Musician Prince died in 2016 without a will. His estate became the subject of a years-long administration and distribution process; a major agreement was reached in 2022. The story illustrates how even substantial estates can face delays and complicated decisions without clear planning.

Read the reporting ↗

The examples illustrate general risks. They do not establish what would happen to a particular family, and probate rules vary across U.S. states and Canadian provinces and territories.

FAQ

Probate questions families often ask.

The answers below are educational, not legal advice.

Does every estate go through probate?

No. Whether probate is required depends on the law, type of assets, ownership and other facts. Ontario, for example, states that probate is not always necessary.

If I have a will, can my estate avoid probate?

Not automatically. A will can direct the distribution of certain assets while a court process may still be necessary.

Does life insurance always bypass probate?

Not always. Beneficiary designations, estate naming, ownership rules and applicable laws matter.

What about probate in Canada?

Estate law is provincial or territorial. Ontario describes probate as a court procedure confirming an estate trustee's authority or validating a will. Other jurisdictions have their own rules.

Can Aurora act as my estate lawyer?

No. Aurora can explain concepts and show reputable sources. Legal advice about your family requires a qualified lawyer in your jurisdiction.
