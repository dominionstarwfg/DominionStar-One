Universal Life Insurance

Understand how flexible-premium permanent coverage works—and why funding, crediting rates and insurance costs deserve ongoing attention.

Explore the Guide ↓Compare All Types →

Explore the essentials

Flexible doesn't mean maintenance-free.

Universal life is a type of permanent insurance that can offer flexibility in premium payments and death benefit options, subject to contractual requirements. Interest credits and ongoing deductions affect how long a policy can remain in force.

  • Cost of insurance and policy fees may change over time under contract rules.
  • Premium flexibility can affect whether the policy remains funded adequately.
  • Interest-crediting terms may vary, and illustrated values should not be treated as guaranteed.
  • Some variants include additional no-lapse guarantees subject to strict conditions.

How it works

Four connected components

Think of universal life as a policy requiring periodic review—not a set-and-forget account.

Premium contributions

Premiums fund the policy; timing and amount influence cash value and coverage sustainability.

Credited interest

Interest may be credited according to the policy's contractual method and guarantees.

Monthly policy charges

Insurance costs and fees are deducted and can erode cash value.

Policy maintenance

Insufficient funding or unfavorable assumptions may cause lapse unless applicable guarantees are satisfied.

What to ask

Contract details that matter

Before discussing a UL product, compare guaranteed and illustrated values.

Can I skip premiums?

Some contracts allow flexibility, but skipping or reducing payments may cause cash value to decline and ultimately lapse.

Does universal life always last for life?

No. Coverage may end if funding is insufficient, unless applicable contract guarantees continue to be satisfied.

Is UL the same as IUL?

IUL is a type of universal life where credited interest may depend on an index-linked formula. It has additional moving parts such as caps and participation rates.

What is a no-lapse guarantee?

Some policies offer guarantees that keep coverage in force subject to specific conditions. Those conditions need contract-by-contract review.
