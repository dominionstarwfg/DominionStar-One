How to evaluate a financial services opportunity

1. Start with licensing

Ask which license is required, which regulator governs it, what education is needed, what fees are involved, and which steps must be completed before conducting regulated activity.

2. Understand the business relationship

Clarify whether the opportunity is employment, independent contracting, commission-based production, leadership development, or a combination. Do not assume the word “career” means salary or employee benefits.

3. Ask about compensation carefully

Request written explanations of compensation, chargebacks, expenses, renewals, advances, eligibility, and factors affecting payment. Reject guaranteed-income claims.

4. Evaluate mentorship

Strong mentorship should include clear expectations, ethical client service, product suitability, compliance, communication, and measurable development—not only motivation.

5. Assess your available time

Licensing study, training, appointments, follow-up, documentation, and continuing education all require consistent time.

6. Review jurisdiction limits

Rules, products, appointments, and contracting vary across U.S. states and Canadian provinces. Confirm requirements for your exact location.

7. Make a deliberate decision

A good discovery process should allow questions, disclosures, time to evaluate, and freedom to decline.
