Resident and non-resident licensing, explained.

Learn the general difference between resident and non-resident licensing and why jurisdiction-specific verification matters.

A resident license is generally connected to the jurisdiction where a professional legally resides. A non-resident license may allow a qualified professional to operate in another jurisdiction, subject to its rules and provider requirements.

Start with the resident jurisdiction

Most professionals first complete the education, exam, application, and related requirements for their resident jurisdiction.

Non-resident licensing is not automatic

Eligibility, fees, reciprocity, background requirements, renewals, appointments, and product access can vary. Always verify current requirements through the applicable regulator and provider.

Why people seek non-resident licenses

Non-resident licensing may support clients or business relationships across jurisdictional lines, but only when the professional is properly licensed, appointed, trained, and authorized.

Important: This article is general educational information. Requirements change and must be verified with the applicable regulator, approved education provider, carrier, and platform at the time you proceed.
