What is a living trust?

Organize ownership and instructions before they are needed.

A revocable living trust is established while the person creating it is alive. It can name a trustee and successor trustee, define instructions for trust property and often be amended or revoked during the creator's lifetime, subject to the document and law. It is different from an irrevocable trust.

01 · CONTROL

During your lifetime

In many revocable arrangements, the creator remains trustee and retains control of assets transferred to the trust, subject to its terms. A successor may manage trust assets following incapacity or death.

02 · DISTRIBUTION

After death

A trustee follows the trust document for covered property. Properly funded trust assets may transfer without a separate probate proceeding, but this is not automatic for every asset.

03 · IMPLEMENTATION

Funding and updates

Retitling a home, updating appropriate accounts or coordinating designated beneficiaries may be necessary. The trust must also be maintained when property or family circumstances change.

Living Trust FAQ

Know the questions worth asking.

Does a living trust always avoid probate?

No. Whether probate is reduced or avoided depends on which assets were properly transferred or otherwise coordinated with the trust and on local rules. Assets outside a trust may still require probate.

Will a revocable trust automatically protect assets from creditors?

Generally, a person who retains control of a revocable trust does not obtain automatic creditor protection simply by creating one. Legal treatment depends on jurisdiction and circumstances.

Do I still need a will?

Often yes. A will may address assets not transferred to the trust, and it can perform other purposes such as naming a guardian for minor children where permitted. Ask qualified counsel how the documents should work together.

Does a living trust save taxes?

Not by itself. A typical revocable living trust generally does not create an automatic income-tax or estate-tax advantage. More sophisticated tax planning, if relevant, requires separate legal and tax guidance.

REAL ESTATE LESSON · MINNESOTA

Prince’s estate: the consequences of missing instructions.

When musician Prince died in 2016, he left no will. The ensuing estate administration and valuation disputes took years. The Associated Press reported a $156.4 million agreed valuation in 2022. This story illustrates why clear planning matters; it does not prove that a living trust would have prevented every dispute or cost.
